## What makes a short

- One story, one point. A five-minute recording usually holds *one* good
  45-second short, not a shortened version of the whole thing.
- Target length is given by the run input (`target_length`); 30–60 s is the
  sweet spot, 61–90 s is the dead zone.
- Voice-over-driven: the recording is b-roll, the VO carries the story. The
  VO is written after the hook is chosen, never before.
- Build is the main dish; a demo is garnish.

## Formats

- `story` — setup, turn, payoff.
- `list` — a number in the hook, that many beats, no more.
- `loop` — the ending feeds the beginning (see below).
- `teardown` — a claim, the evidence, the reframe.

## Hooks

- ≤ 12 words. The first two seconds decide everything; the title is a
  separate job.
- Mechanisms: number, contradiction, result-first, question, confession.
  Vary them across the options.
- Confident, contrarian but not controversial. Never self-deprecating.

## Loop shorts

Split the opening sentence. The video starts on its second half and ends on
its first half. Record the joined sentence in one breath and cut it in the
edit so the seam inherits natural intonation. Visual seam: the closing
clip's source range must end exactly where the opening clip's begins.

## Captions

Sentence case, one short phrase per cue, driven by the VO word times. Fix
proper nouns and URLs against the approved script — the transcription
carries errors through.

## Music moods

`cooking` for payoff and working demos · `curiosity` for exploration ·
`tension` for debugging · `resolution` for closings — never under a loop
short, since a loop must not resolve. Keep music about −15 to −18 dB under
the VO. Non-resolution tracks are loopable; check the beat math against the
short's length.
